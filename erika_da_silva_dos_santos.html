Sobre

Erika Santos is Assistant Professor in Universidade de Lisboa - Instituto Superior de Agronomia (Portugal). With a background in Biology and Scientific Master in Nature Conservation and Management, she has a European PhD in Environmental Engineering (Universidade de Lisboa and Universidad de Santiago de Compostela from Spain) followed by post-doctoral grant on Agricultural and Food Biotechnology at CICECO - Aveiro Institute of Materials and Universidad de Santiago de Compostela. She has more than 20 years of academic experience as lecturer, in which she taught in several curricular units of Degree, Master and professional courses from Portugal, Spain, Perú and Bolivia, and developed other academic functions. She has been advisor of 2 pos-PhD students, 2 PhD student, >15 MSc students, 26 BSc students and 7 students in technical formation as well as several works of students (BSc, MSc, PhD) and researchers in international mobility (e.g. Spain, Italy, Peru, Argelia, Netherlands, Germany, Brazil). She participated, as coordinator (PI) and researcher, in several R&D projects (co)-financed by European/International and Portuguese funds, and companies. Nowadays, she has published 7 international chapters’ books, >50 articles in international indexes journals, 16 articles in national journals, >35 proceedings, and 2 technical books. She participated in several national/international technical and scientific congresses/seminars for presentations in the form of oral communication (by invitation: 17, speaker: >70) or poster (>100) as well as Editor or Editorial Coordinator of 3 books (3) and committee organizer. She has an h-index of 23. Her main interests and experience covers the physico-chemical and biological evaluation of different soil types (agricultural, forestry, urban, industrial), biogeochemistry, environmental recovery of degraded or contaminated areas with nature-based solutions (e.g. Technosols, phytostabilization with autochthones plants, biochar); soil-plant and soil-microrganism interaction; and valorisation of endogenous resources (e.g. plants used in the recovered systems by their natural value-added compounds).
